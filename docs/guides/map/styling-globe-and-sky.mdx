The [Styling module](./styling.mdx)'s `view` knobs own the map-level MapLibre state that a style switch would otherwise reset — projection and sky — so a globe stays a globe across `setStyle`. They are knobs like any other: `set`, `get`, `reset` and `getConfig()` carry them, and they work on any style, custom ones included.

<GuideDemo demo="globe-terrain" />

## Projection and sky

```javascript
const styling = await StylingModule.get(map);

styling.set('view.projection', 'globe');
styling.set('view.sky', true);        // MapLibre's default sky is fully transparent: a globe with no atmosphere
```

The `globe` [preset](./styling.mdx#presets) sets both in one call. 3D terrain is not a knob: it needs the elevation style part loaded first, which [`TerrainModule.get`](./terrain.mdx#3d-elevation) does for you.

## Sky colours

The sky follows the style's light/dark theme: a daylit blue with a white horizon over a light style, a night blue with a dim horizon over a dark one. `view.skyColor` and `view.horizonColor` override either, and a style that declares its own `sky` wins over both.

## The colour behind the map

`view.spaceColor` is the colour **behind** the map — what a globe leaves visible around the planet. The map canvas is transparent there, so something has to fill it. Where your page already gives the map container a background, that colour is the knob's default and what a `reset` returns to, so the SDK never paints over your own styling. Where it does not, the knob follows the style theme as the sky does, and is re-applied after a `setStyle`, so switching light to dark carries the surround with it:

```javascript
styling.set('view.spaceColor', '#05070d');   // or leave it to follow the style theme
```

## What to expect

Three things worth knowing, measured rather than assumed:

- The globe flattens to Mercator between zoom 11 and 12 by design.
- Without `view.sky` a globe has no atmosphere.
- MapLibre's fog over a tilted view starts at pitch 60, which is also its default `maxPitch`, so raise `maxPitch` on the map if you want to see it.

The demo above zooms a globe into 3D terrain; the [3D terrain playground](../../examples/terrain-playground) keeps a tilted camera, its sky and its relief across a style switch. The [map effects plugin](../plugins/map-effects-depth-of-field.mdx) adds a depth of field over a tilted view.
